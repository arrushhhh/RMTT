    Case

    BreatheSmart Asthma Support App

    A university clinic is considering BreatheSmart, a mobile app for students aged 18 to 25 with physician-diagnosed asthma. The app sends controller-medication reminders and personalised air-quality warnings based on phone location. It records whether reminders are opened, while students enter missed doses and symptoms.

    The proposed eight-week study would recruit 80 volunteers. One group would receive the app and the other would receive the clinic's standard information sheet. Planned data include app logs, self-reported missed doses, a weekly validated symptom-control score, baseline asthma severity and digital-literacy score.

    The team currently calls the project "Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare" and asks, "Does technology improve asthma?" They also write, "The app will definitely improve patients' health." The literature folder contains several records suggested by a generative AI tool, but some records have no DOI and have not been opened. The app would collect location data continuously unless the participant changes the default setting.

    Use these summaries as the literature base. No external knowledge about asthma is expected.
